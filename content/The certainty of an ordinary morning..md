title: The certainty of an ordinary morning.
Brief: Although written in casual, free-flowing prose, this piece captures quite well the chain of events that unfold on an ordinary morning. It explores the mind-body connection and how intrusive thoughts, self-punishment, and certain ways of thinking can trigger a visceral, even animalistic, physical reaction.

Beginning with boredom,  
followed by a reluctance to exist,  
and then a drift into the dreams of a waking mind,  
it is in this jumble of ideas that a way out takes shape once again,  
a powerful imagination that longs for a slightly better day.  

Even if the opposite were desired,  
the recounting of the past is inevitable.  
Even having reached hope,  
one eventually lands in a swarm of memories.  
What was and what wasn’t yesterday,  
what could have been and what refused to be.  

Once past the forest of ideas, one reaches the lake.  
Deep waters tasting of disappointment,  
the oars stir, and amid grimaces, the boat is pushed forward.  

Even in this place, the damned mosquitoes exist,  
striking when least expected,  
making one bleed with past tortures,  
and fraying the nerves, forcing one to remember.  
But we must not forget where these damned flies come from:  
They are still us.  
Just as they bite, they confuse.  
I no longer know if it’s me… but what if it is?  
Is self-flagellation enough redemption?  

The physical resentment of the mental battle makes itself felt.  
It attacks subtly, for it begins little by little.  

While the arms sink into the cushion,  
the footrests greet the stiffened toes.  
And so, all tense, they wait.  
Welcome, Mrs. Discomfort.  

This isn’t ordinary discouragement.  
It’s hatred of the flesh,  
a revolt of the veins,  
an unrelenting mental itch.  
Think of it as stirring up a beehive.  

The sack of bones writhes between the sheets at dawn,  
staggering from side to side on the mattress.  
The springs creak and echo like whimpers from their mechanical strain.  

Thus, amid the body’s groans,  
the desire to escape is expressed,  
but we must not forget the mind.  
   
Already, left to itself, it began to mutilate itself,  
yet even so, it does not wish to get up.  
They are not adversaries;  
however, one cannot cry inwardly.  

It is drenched in disappointment.  
Because of the extra weight, it’s inevitable to feel trapped—  
look how she hides and how she flees!  
But something inside her knows:  
in the midst of despair,  
the body lurks with a carnivorous intent.  
At last, these two agree on something!  

The feet get the floor.
